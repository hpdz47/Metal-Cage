# Case 5 – Pricing a New AI Wearable Device

**Question 6**

**Recommendation**

I recommend Strategy B, launching NovaBand at $599.

3. The price balances premium positioning with customer demand, allowing the company to maximise profitability without pricing beyond the value customers currently perceive.

**Risks**

- Sales volume and pre-order conversion
- Customer acquisition and repeat purchase intent
- Customer feedback on perceived value and AI features
- Gross margin
- Competitor product launches and pricing responses

If adoption is weaker than expected, the client should refine the value proposition, strengthen marketing around the AI capabilities, or revisit the pricing strategy before expanding the launch.

  - Retailer pricing behaviour and shelf placement

- positions Ultra credibly within the premium wearable market.

While a $599 launch would maximise unit sales, it risks excessive migration from the Pro, reducing overall portfolio profitability. Conversely, $799 significantly limits cannibalisation but also reduces demand and weakens category growth.

Launching at $699 preserves a clear Good–Better–Best product hierarchy while maximising long-term portfolio value.

**Question 7**

I would **not** immediately change Active or Pro pricing.

Instead: Maintain the product ladder.

- Good: Active
- ↓
- Better: Pro
- ↓
- Best: Ultra

After observing customer behaviour, consider:

- Reducing Pro slightly if Ultra adoption is slower than expected,
- Repositioning Pro around a different feature set,
- OR eventually retiring Pro if Ultra fully replaces it.

Immediate repricing could create unnecessary confusion.

**Final Recommendation**

I recommend a multi-layered pricing strategy rather than a single price.

First, adopt regional pricing to reflect differences in purchasing power, competitive intensity and brand strength across markets. The Ultra should command a higher price in mature premium markets such as the United States and Germany, while more competitive introductory pricing may be appropriate in Thailand and India to accelerate adoption.

Second, maintain a Good–Better–Best portfolio:

- Volt Lite for price-sensitive customers,
- Volt Pro for mainstream commuters,
- Volt Ultra AI as the flagship innovation product.

Third, include essential AI capabilities in the purchase price while offering Advanced coaching, predictive maintenance and premium software features through a monthly subscription. This creates recurring revenue without creating a high barrier to initial adoption.

Finally, use targeted promotional pricing rather than permanent discounts to respond to competitive pressure or stimulate demand in specific markets or customer segments.

This integrated strategy best balances the client's objectives of profitability, growth, premium positioning and recurring revenue.
