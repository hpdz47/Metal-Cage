## Five Steps to Tackle Pricing Strategy Cases

Strong candidates follow a logical, hypothesis-driven approach rather than random analysis

1. **Step 1: Clarify The Objective & Define Success**
2. **Step 2: Understand the Business Situation (Company, Product, Customer, Competition)**
3. **Step 3: The Three Pricing Lenses (3Cs) / Balance cost, competition and customer value (not just one pricing method)**
4. **Step 4: Put It Together (Pricing Triangle) / Quantitative Analysis**
5. **Step 5: Deliver a CEO-ready recommendation supported by evidence, risks and next steps**

## Case 1 — Premium Coffee Shop Launch  [Easy]

**Recommendation**

I recommend launching the drink at 179 THB.

This recommendation aligns with the client's objective of maximising long-term profitability while maintaining its premium positioning. There are three reasons for this:

1. The drink is differentiated through premium ingredients and presentation, supporting a premium price.
2. Customer research indicates that purchase intent remains strong at 179 THB, while demand falls meaningfully at 200 THB and above.
3. The proposed price sits comfortably alongside Starbucks and other premium competitors without attempting to undercut them, while generating an attractive contribution margin of approximately 94 THB per cup.

Hence, I recommend launching the drink at 179 THB.

---

## Case 1 — Premium Coffee Shop Launch  [Easy]

However, there are two key risks:
- Actual willingness to pay may be lower than expected, resulting in lower sales volumes if customer behaviour differs from the research findings.
- Competitive responses, such as promotional pricing or the launch of similar premium beverages, could reduce demand and put pressure on market share.

It is also important to note that premium positioning does not necessarily mean charging the highest possible price. Pricing the drink at 220 THB could significantly reduce demand, create poor value perception, encourage customers to switch to competitors, and slow product adoption.

**Next Steps**

Following launch, I would recommend closely monitoring:
- Sales volume
- Repeat purchase rate
- Customer feedback and satisfaction
- Gross margin
- Competitor pricing and promotional activity

If demand is stronger than expected, the client should consider a gradual price increase while monitoring its impact on volume and customer sentiment. Conversely, if demand is weaker than expected, the client should investigate the underlying drivers and refine the pricing strategy or value proposition before scaling nationally.

## Case 2 – Launching a New Sports Drink  [Easy]

I recommend launching at 25 THB.

This pricing strikes an appropriate balance between customer adoption, competitive positioning and long-term profitability.

## Case 3 – Pricing a SaaS Platform  [Intermediate]

**Question 7**

**Recommendation**

I recommend introducing a three-tier subscription model:
- Basic – $9/month for freelancers and price-sensitive users.
- Professional – $16/month for SMEs, which are the largest and most valuable customer segment.
- Enterprise – Custom pricing for large organisations requiring Advanced security, analytics and dedicated support.

This recommendation best aligns with the client's objective of maximising long-term recurring revenue while accelerating adoption.

A tiered model captures the different willingness to pay across customer segments, remains competitive against existing SaaS providers, and creates a clear upgrade path as customers grow.

With a variable cost of only $4 per customer per month, all three tiers generate healthy contribution margins while increasing pricing flexibility.
